Factoring is known to be a difficult problem, (<a style="font-size:small" href="https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/" target="_blank">Citation needed</a>).
The numbers seem unwilling to divulge any non-trivial factors, much like a recalcitrant prisoner might obstinately refuse to answer a detective's
queries to avoid implicating themselves.<!--more-->
<img src="/sloi/img/fact/gcd/gcd1.jpg" style="max-width:75%"/>

There are tons of resources about the [Greatest Common Denominator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_common_divisor) (GCD),
and why it works, so I won't seek to improve on that.
However, what's often absent is just how awesome and useful the GCD actually is:  Literally one of Math's powertools.

So, in appreciation of this, I present the following meme:
<br/>
<h2>Detective Euclid cracks another case</h2>
<img src="/sloi/img/fact/gcd/gcd2.jpg" style="max-width:75%"/>


It seems amazing to me, given that factoring is such a difficult problem, (<a style="font-size:small" href="https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/" target="_blank">Citation needed</a>)
With a number seemingly unwilling to divulge any non-trivial factors.  But, simply ask about another number ala the GCD, and it'll happily spill the beans on largest factor it might have in common.
