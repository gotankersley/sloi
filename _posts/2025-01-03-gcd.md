The numbers seem unwilling to divulge any non-trivial factors, much like a recalcitrant prisoner might obstinately refuse to answer a detective's
queries to avoid implicating themselves...<!--more-->

<img src="/sloi/img/fact/gcd/gcd1.jpg" style="max-width:75%"/>

<h2>Detective Euclid cracks another case</h2>

But, our detective is clever, crafty and cunning fellow.  So, instead of asking directly, he simply inquires about another number:

<img src="/sloi/img/fact/gcd/gcd2.jpg" style="max-width:75%"/>

And, just like that, asking about another number, (via the GCD), and the factorix will happily spill the beans on largest factor it might have in common.

*Case Closed, Banana Boy...*

There are, of course, tons of resources about the venerable [Greatest Common Denominator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_common_divisor) (GCD) algorithm, and how and why it works, so I won't seek to improve on that.

However, what's often absent is just how awesome and useful the GCD actually is.  Literally one of Math's great powertools.
